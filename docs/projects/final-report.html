Final Report: [Obesity]

Introduction

Obesity has emerged as a major public health problem. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) reported the prevalence rate of obesity in the United States as 40.3% during 2021 - 2023 period [1]. Unlike acute illnesses, the health consequences of obesity typically develop gradually. Obesity accelerates biological aging by promoting chronic low-grade inflammation, oxidative stress, and mitochondrial dysfunction, all of which contribute to the earlier onset of age-related diseases [2]. It increases the risk of numerous chronic conditions, including type 2 diabetes mellitus, cardiovascular disease, metabolic syndrome, hypertension, and certain types of cancers [3, 4]. In this project I hypothesize that healthier lifestyle habits are associated with lower obesity rates. The findings may contribute to a better understanding of the problem and alter public health strategies.

Methods

Obesity Prediction dataset was used in this project. It is available on Kaggle: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/ruchikakumbhar/obesity-prediction

The dataset included 2,111 participants and 17 variables related to demographics, lifestyle, and obesity level.

# The Chi-square test also found a significant association between obesity and consumption of high-calorie foods (χ² = 233.34, p < 0.001). High-calorie food intake was more common among participants in the obesity categories.

Conclusions

The results suggest that obesity is influenced by several factors. Participants with higher obesity levels generally had lower physical activity, were more likely to report a family history of obesity, and more frequently consumed high-calorie foods.

Limitation of this study is that the dataset is observational, so it cannot prove cause and effect. In addition, some obesity groups contained very few participants of one gender, making those comparisons less reliable.
